# Nachtzug’s licences, in plain words

Nachtzug is free. You may use it, share it and change it, and you never have to ask. This page explains in plain words what the licences allow; the licence texts themselves are what count, and they’re linked below.

## The music you make is yours

Everything you make with Nachtzug belongs to you, completely: your songs, your patterns, your sounds and every WAV or MIDI file you export. You can release it, sell it, put it in a game or a film, and you don’t have to mention Nachtzug (though I’d be delighted if you did!).

That goes for the built-in sounds, too. The sample library, the demo songs and the starter songs are given away with no strings attached, so use them in your music however you like.

## Made with AI: what it means for copyright

Much of Nachtzug’s code and many of its texts were written by AI tools, which a person directed, checked and corrected. That has a consequence worth saying plainly. Copyright protects only what people create: German law protects a work only as a “persönliche geistige Schöpfung”, a personal intellectual creation (§ 2 (2) UrhG, and for computer programs § 69a (3) UrhG), and the US Copyright Office takes the same view. Code or text that an AI tool produced without a person shaping it may therefore not be protected by copyright at all. Which parts of Nachtzug are protected, and how far, has never been tested, and nobody can say it line by line.

What that means in practice:

- The licences apply to everything in Nachtzug that is protected: the program as a whole, its design and how its parts fit together, and the texts and pictures a person wrote, chose or reworked.
- Whatever is not protected is free anyway: nobody, its maker included, can forbid anyone to use it.
- For you, nothing changes. Follow the licences as they stand, and you are on the safe side for every part.
- No more rights are claimed than the law gives, and nothing is promised beyond what the licences say.

None of this touches the music you make with Nachtzug: you made it, and it is yours.

## About the cover painting

The cover painting is Hans Baluschek’s “Großstadtbahnhof” (1904), which is in the public domain; the only copy to be found was small, so it was enlarged with an AI upscaler (4xRealWebPhoto) and its colours were freshened a little.
