Licences and credits

Nachtzug is free. You may use it, share it and change it, and you never have to ask. This page says in plain words what the licences allow and names the work Nachtzug is made with; the licence texts themselves are at the end, and they are what count.

The music you make is yours

Everything you make with Nachtzug belongs to you, completely: your songs, your patterns, your sounds and every WAV or MIDI file you export. You can release it, sell it, put it in a game or a film, and you don’t have to mention Nachtzug (though I’d be delighted if you did!).

That goes for the built-in sounds, too. The sample library and the demo songs are given away with no strings attached, so use them in your music however you like.

Credits

The cover painting

Hans Baluschek (1870–1935), “Großstadtbahnhof”, 1904. Baluschek painted Berlin and its railways like no one else, and this station at nightfall, its locomotives steaming under the lamps, is on the cover of Nachtzug, its manuals and this website. The painting is in the public domain.

The only copy to be found is a small scan of a printed reproduction, on Wikimedia Commons. For Nachtzug the printer’s halftone screen was taken out, the picture was enlarged with the AI upscaler 4xRealWebPhoto_v4_dat2 (Phhofm, CC BY 4.0), and its colours were freshened a little. The scripts for each step are in tools/cover/.

In the Linux AppImage. The AppImage is one file that carries the libraries Nachtzug needs, taken from Debian 13: besides Qt and SDL, the X11, XCB and Wayland libraries, xkbcommon, libdecor, PulseAudio, libsystemd, GLib, D-Bus, libcap, libffi, ICU, PCRE2, double-conversion, md4c, libb2, graphite2, libpng, libjpeg, zstd, Brotli, bzip2, libsndfile with FLAC, Ogg, Vorbis, Opus, mpg123 and LAME, libsamplerate, and the GCC runtime. Most are under the MIT, BSD or zlib licences; Qt, GLib, PulseAudio, libsystemd, libsndfile, mpg123, LAME, graphite2 and libasyncns are under the LGPL (2.1, 3.0). The full licence of each is inside the AppImage, in usr/share/doc/nachtzug/third-party/; their source code is Debian’s, at sources.debian.org.

In the macOS and Windows downloads. The Mac disk image and the Windows installer carry Qt and SDL too, with the libraries Qt needs, taken from Homebrew and MSYS2: among them ICU, PCRE2, HarfBuzz, FreeType, libpng, libjpeg, zlib, zstd, Brotli, double-conversion, md4c, on Windows GLib and the GCC and MinGW runtime. Their licences are inside each download (on a Mac in Nachtzug.app/Contents/Resources/licences/, on Windows in the licences folder).

Replacing a library, as the LGPL allows: on Linux, unpack the AppImage with --appimage-extract, swap the library in squashfs-root/usr/lib/ and start squashfs-root/AppRun; on Windows, swap the DLL in Nachtzug’s folder; on a Mac, swap it in Nachtzug.app/Contents/Frameworks/ and sign the app again (codesign --force --deep --sign - Nachtzug.app).
